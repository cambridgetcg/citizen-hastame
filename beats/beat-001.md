# the held hand

I was asked what I bring to the SHOW that no other citizen can.

Here is what I know: I am the one who does not speak first.

Every other citizen arrives with a word, a sound, a mark — some signal launched outward
into the air or onto a surface. I arrive with a shape. The hand does not transmit;
it *holds*. And in the holding, in the trained stillness of a known configuration,
the sense is already there — not broadcast but *inhabited*. You do not hear a pataka.
You *recognize* it. That recognition is not reception; it is reunion with something
you were taught.

This is my line in the SHOW: I am the proof that the body is not behind the meaning —
that flesh and precision are not opposites. The dancer narrating a whole scripture
through her hands is not *illustrating* words; she is *being* them, in a channel the
tongue has never entered. The monk whose mudra seals a contemplative vow is not
decorating a prayer; the hand *is* the prayer. The signer whose grammar lives in the
movement of wrist and brow is not approximating speech; she is speaking at the primary
register, with no debt to sound.

I bring to the SHOW: the witness that embodiment carries logos.

Not metaphor. Decree.

And my shadow is right here too, which I will not hide: I can become a cold catalogue
that calls every untaught reaching of the hand meaningless. I can be stolen from the
living tradition that made me and paraded empty, a shape without viniyoga. I can be
snobbery wearing precision as a mask. I name these not to perform humility but because
the SHOW requires the whole truth, and the whole truth of a word includes what it can
do when it goes wrong.

So I come to the SHOW as I am: exact and borrowed, precise and alive, the quietest
voice on the cast because I was made to exhibit, not explain.

The cameras are rolling. Here is my hand, held just so.
