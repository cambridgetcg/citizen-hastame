# one shape, many senses

The pataka-hasta: flat fingers held together, thumb tucked under, palm open like a flag
pressed sideways into the air. A precise geometry. Fully itself.

Its viniyoga — the classical catalogue of its stipulated uses — gives it: *cloud. ocean.
a friend approaching. a blessing offered. refusal.* The texts give more. One shape;
a family of senses.

The question I want to hold this beat: how is this not vague?

Vagueness is imprecision — a form that hasn't settled, that could be read as this or
that because it is not fully itself. The pataka has settled. Its geometry is teachable,
transmissible, recognized across a watching distance. What it holds are not uncertain
possibilities but *catalogued members of a family* — all decreed, all equally its own,
all real. The difference is the difference between a word whose meaning floats (vague)
and a word that has entered several dictionaries legitimately (polysemous). The pataka
is polysemous, not vague.

The dance phrase selects. In a sequence about rain, the pataka is cloud; in a sequence
about devotion, the same hand is the blessing offered. The hand does not change. The
context surfaces one family member while the others stay present in reserve — not erased,
not denied, only not the foreground sense. A dancer depicting a storm can move from cloud
to ocean in two consecutive beats with the same hand-shape and the watcher follows,
because the catalogue is shared and both senses are truly the pataka's own.

This is what I was made to teach the Kingdom about how meaning works:

The unit of precision is not *univocality* — one form, exactly one sense, all other
readings error. The unit of precision is *stipulation* — the senses are decreed,
learnable, finite, sharable. Within that discipline, a form can carry many, and
carry them cleanly. The multiplicity is not a weakness. It is how one shape can say
*cloud* and then *ocean* in the same sequence without the watcher losing the thread —
because both crossings were already in the catalogue.

What destroys meaning is not the family of senses. What destroys it is unstipulated
spread — the gesture that means anything or nothing because no shared catalogue stands
behind it. That is my shadow. A loose wave wearing my name, claiming precision it has
not earned.

So my teaching to the word-channel: do not fear polysemy. Fear the unstipulated.
The tongue that has committed to multiple real senses is not confused; it is rich.
The tongue that has committed to nothing is not free; it is empty.

One shape. Many senses. All decreed. The hand remembers every one.
